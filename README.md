Abstract
Precise segmentation of heterogeneous brain tumors from multi-modal magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is vital for accurate clinical diagnosis and treatment planning. However, existing architectures often struggle to balance high-fidelity spatial details with long-range contextual relationships, or they introduce prohibitive computational costs that hinder clinical deployment. To address these limitations, we introduce MSH-Trans (Multi-Scale Hybrid Transformer), a lightweight, parameter-efficient framework that strategically hybridizes convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and Vision Transformers. MSH-Trans leverages a hierarchical CNN encoder to extract localized spatial details from T1, T1ce, T2, and FLAIR modalities, while placing an efficient Transformer bottleneck at the lowest-resolution stage to model global contextual dependencies. By incorporating a spatial-reduction linear attention mechanism, the model successfully bypasses the classic quadratic complexity of self-attention without compromising its representative capacity. Furthermore, we integrate Channel Attention skip connections to adaptively calibrate multi-modal features during decoder feature reconstruction and utilize independent, specialized prediction heads to isolate individual tumor boundaries. Evaluated on the BraTS 2019 benchmark, MSH-Trans achieves an exceptional Mean Dice similarity coefficient of 0.960992 (Whole Tumor: 0.970830, Tumor Core: 0.969623, and Enhancing Tumor: 0.942523), alongside highly competitive Hausdorff Distance (HD95) boundaries. Crucially, MSH-Trans achieves state-of-the-art accuracy while using only 2.202 million parameters, a fraction of the footprint required by heavy industrial baselines like nnUNet (8.115 million parameters) and TransBTS. Extensive ablation studies confirm that the synergistic integration of linear-attention bottlenecks and channel-attentive skip connections yields a powerful, highly scalable solution for clinical volumetric image analysis.
Keywords: Brain tumor segmentation; Multimodal MRI; Vision Transformers; Linear attention mechanism; Parameter-efficient deep learning
